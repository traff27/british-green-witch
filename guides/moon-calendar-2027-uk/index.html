2027 moon calendar for the UK: every new and full moon

Last updated: 23 November 2026. All times are UK clock time (GMT or BST, as the clocks stand on the day).

The short answer. There are 12 full moons and 13 new moons in 2027. The first full moon is on Friday 22 January at 12:17 GMT and the last on Monday 13 December at 16:08 GMT. Two eclipses can be seen from the UK: a faint penumbral eclipse of the Moon on the night of 20 February, and a partial eclipse of the Sun on the morning of Monday 2 August.

About the moon names

You will see names such as Wolf Moon, Snow Moon and Strawberry Moon all over the internet. They are lovely, but they are not old British names. Almost all of them were popularised by twentieth-century North American almanacs, which drew on, and sometimes invented around, Native American and colonial American sources. We list them above because people search for them, and we label them for what they are.

Two names are genuinely at home in English: the Harvest Moon and the Hunter’s Moon, which have been used in Britain for centuries for the full moons of early autumn.

The oldest English names we actually have are for the months, not the moons. The monk Bede, writing in Northumbria in 725, recorded the names the pagan English used before they became Christian:

  • January: Æfterra Gēola, “after Yule”. February: Sōlmōnaþ, which Bede explains as the month of cakes offered to the gods.
  • March: Hrēþmōnaþ, after a goddess Hreda. April: Ēosturmōnaþ, after the goddess Eostre, the source of the word Easter.
  • May: Þrimilcemōnaþ, “three-milkings month”. June and July: Ǣrra Līþa and Æfterra Līþa, “before” and “after” Litha.
  • August: Wēodmōnaþ, “weed month”. September: Hāligmōnaþ, “holy month”.
  • October: Winterfylleþ, from winter and the full moon. November: Blōtmōnaþ, “month of sacrifice”. December: Ǣrra Gēola, “before Yule”.

You are just as welcome to name each moon after what you see from your own window: the first frost, the curlews coming back, the elder in flower.

Blue moon, Harvest Moon and Hunter’s Moon in 2027

  • Seasonal blue moon, Thursday 20 May (11:59 BST). No month in 2027 has two full moons, so there is no calendar blue moon. But spring has four full moons (22 March, 20 April, 20 May and 19 June), and by the older almanac rule the third full moon in a season of four is the blue moon.
  • Harvest Moon, Thursday 16 September (00:03 BST). The full moon nearest the autumn equinox. For a few evenings around it the Moon rises at almost the same time each night, giving extra light at dusk.
  • Hunter’s Moon, Friday 15 October (14:47 BST). The full moon after the Harvest Moon. Look for it rising on the evenings of the 14th and 15th.
  • Two new moons in August, on 2 and 31 August. The first coincides with the solar eclipse.

Eclipses visible from the UK in 2027

There are four eclipses worldwide in 2027. Two can be seen from the British Isles.

Penumbral lunar eclipse: night of Saturday 20 February

The full moon passes through the pale outer part of the Earth’s shadow. From London the eclipse begins at 21:12 GMT, is greatest at 23:12 GMT and ends at 01:13 GMT on Sunday 21 February, with the Moon high in the south. A penumbral eclipse is subtle: look for a faint grey shading across one side of the Moon around 23:00. It is not the dramatic red moon of a total eclipse. Times are essentially the same across the UK.

Partial solar eclipse: morning of Monday 2 August

On 2 August the Moon passes in front of the Sun. The eclipse is total only along a narrow track across southern Spain, North Africa and Egypt. From the UK it is partial: the Moon takes a bite out of the Sun for about two hours in the middle of the morning. The further south and west you are, the bigger the bite.

Watch the eclipse safely

Never look directly at the Sun, even when much of it is covered. Ordinary sunglasses, smoked glass, exposed film and phone screens do not protect your eyes, and damage to the retina can happen without pain. Binoculars or a telescope without a proper front-mounted solar filter will cause instant, permanent harm.

Use new, undamaged eclipse glasses or viewers marked ISO 12312-2, from a reputable seller, or watch indirectly with a pinhole projector: a small hole in a piece of card casts an image of the Sun onto a second card. The gaps between leaves or the holes in a colander will throw dozens of little crescent suns onto the ground.

The eclipses you will not see from the UK

The annular solar eclipse of 6 February 2027 is visible from parts of South America and Africa, not from Britain. Two very slight penumbral lunar eclipses, on 18 July and 17 August, happen while the Moon is below the horizon in the UK.

Using the calendar

Many witches use the new moon for beginnings and intentions, and the full moon for gratitude, celebration or a monthly esbat. That is a modern practice, and you do not need permission or a set ritual to follow it. A candle, a walk under the full moon or a few lines in a journal are enough.

If you garden by the Moon, treat it as traditional lore rather than proven science: there is little good evidence that sowing by the Moon’s phase improves crops, but plenty of gardeners enjoy the rhythm it gives the year. Questions

Are these moon times in UK time?

Yes. Every time on this page is UK clock time: GMT from 1 January to 28 March and from 31 October to the end of the year, and BST (GMT plus one hour) in between. You do not need to add or subtract anything.

How many full moons are there in 2027?

Twelve, one in each calendar month. There are thirteen new moons, because August has two (2 and 31 August).

Is there a blue moon in 2027?

Not a calendar blue moon, as no month has two full moons. There is a seasonal blue moon on 20 May 2027: spring has four full moons that year, and by the older almanac rule the third of four in a season is the blue moon. It will not look blue.

When is the Harvest Moon in 2027?

The full moon of Thursday 16 September 2027, exact at 00:03 BST, is the Harvest Moon, because it is the full moon closest to the autumn equinox on 23 September. Look for it rising in the east around sunset on the 15th and 16th.

Can I see the 2 August 2027 solar eclipse from the UK?

Yes, as a partial eclipse, weather permitting. It is total only along a narrow path across southern Spain, North Africa and Egypt. From the UK the Moon covers part of the Sun between about 09:00 and 11:00 BST. Never look at the Sun directly: use certified eclipse glasses or a pinhole projector.
