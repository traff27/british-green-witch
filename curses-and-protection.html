The British Witch's Book of Curses and Protection

Hexes, Unwitching and Protective Charms in British Folklore and Modern Practice

For two thousand years, people in these islands have feared curses and found ways to turn them aside.

From the Roman curse tablets of Bath to the witch bottles hidden under Stuart hearths, the daisy wheels carved by old doorways and the cunning folk who “unwitched” their neighbours, Britain has one of the richest histories of cursing and protection in the world. The British Witch's Book of Curses and Protection follows it honestly, separating what is recorded from what is adapted and what is modern, and shows you how to protect your home and your peace without fear.

Inside the book

  • Before you call it a curse: ordinary explanations, practical safety, where to get real help, and how to spot the curse-removal scam
  • The history: curse tablets, church curses, the witch trials, witch bottles, concealed shoes, ritual marks and the cunning folk of England, Scotland and Wales
  • 41 step-by-step workings for grounding, shielding, home protection, cleansing, cord-cutting, uncrossing and release, each with cheap everyday materials, safety notes and a no-tools version
  • Hearth, door and threshold: a safe modern threshold jar, rowan and red thread crosses, daisy wheels and blessings for a new home
  • Twenty protective hedgerow and garden plants, from rowan and holly to St John's wort and vervain

Deity-optional and suitable for beginners, this is not a revenge spellbook. Every working aims to protect, release and rebuild, never to harm.

By Mark Hutchinson. Part of the Modern Witchcraft & Wicca series.
